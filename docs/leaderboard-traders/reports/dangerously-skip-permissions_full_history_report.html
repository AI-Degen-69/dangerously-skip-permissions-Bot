The edge is real.
It also has a size limit.

The wallet's entire history — 16 weeks, 1,191,338 rows, 31,506 markets. Over the whole sample the trading edge is +87.7 bps and statistically unambiguous (t = 7.97). It is also not a constant: it falls almost linearly with the dollars the wallet puts into each market, from +812 bps at $114/market to −2 bps at $743. That curve, not the average, is the finding.

  • Profitable and significant.Trading net of fees earned $161,292 (+87.7 bps); rebates added $168,080. A weekly-block bootstrap puts the edge at [+47, +140] bps with P(edge ≤ 0) below 0.001.
  • The rebate is a tier, not a constant.Polymarket pays a share of taker fees that depends on 30-day weighted volume, from 3% to 50%. This wallet climbed four rungs during the sample, which is why rebates rose while trading fell — see §04.
  • But the parameter moves.Net trading bps fell from +457 in July to +0.7 in the first eight days of November, a trend of −33 bps per week (t = −6.2). A pooled average over that period describes no period in it.
  • Size explains most of it.Weekly edge regressed on dollars deployed per market gives edge = 464 − 0.620 × ($/market), t = −4.21, R² = 0.54. The wallet went from $114 to $784 per market and got proportionally less per dollar.
  • The two candidate causes are indistinguishable here.Size grew monotonically with time, so "the wallet became too big for the available liquidity" and "the market got more efficient" fit the identical regression. Distinguishing them needs another operator of a different size in the same window.

The capacity curve

Edge as a function of size

Fit is OLS across the 17 weekly observations, weighted equally. The shaded zone left of the data is where the bot actually operates.

The interpretation that matters.An edge that halves when you double your clip is not a signal problem, it is a liquidity problem. The wallet compensated for shrinking bps by growing notional 6×, which is exactly the wrong move: total dollar profit per market stayed flat while the risk per dollar rose.
Why the fit is trustworthy but the extrapolation is not.R² = 0.54 with t = −4.21 on 17 points is a solid relationship. But every observation sits between $114 and $784 per market, so the line's value at the bot's $30–50 is an extrapolation, not a measurement. It says "the small-size regime was the good one", not "you will earn 440 bps".

04

The rebate is a tier, not a constant

Polymarket's Taker Rebate Program is a ladder priced on 30-day weighted volume, not a fixed refund. The wallet climbed four rungs inside this sample — and that climb, not any improvement in trading, is why the rebate rose while net trading fell.

Four observed rates land exactly on four published tier values — that is the whole explanation for the rebound in rebate yield. The lone 0.16 on Jul 21 is unexplained: it sits between Silver and Gold and coincides with a two-day gap in trading, so it is either a brief mid-day tier change or a day-pairing artifact.

  • Volume, not skill, sets the refund.wV is per fill: size × (1 − price) × category weight, with crypto weighted highest at 2.3. For this wallet at ~55% taker share that works out to roughly 1.5× notional.
  • This is a treadmill.Holding Obsidian means turning over roughly $6.5–7M of 30-day notional. Drop below it and the rate falls 6 points, costing ~30 bps on every dollar traded. The volume needed to keep the tier is the same volume that destroys the edge.
  • It also explains the decay.At 8% you keep 92% of your fee burden; at 50% you keep half. Trades that were unprofitable at Silver become profitable at Obsidian — and those marginal trades are, by construction, the worst ones. The ladder subsidises exactly the behaviour that flattened gross edge.
What this does to every number above.The +123 bps of rebate is an Obsidian figure. The same fee base at Gold would yield ~43 bps, at Silver ~19 bps, at Bronze ~7 bps. A $1,000-bankroll bot doing a few hundred dollars of notional a day sits in Bronze or Silver: to reach Gold it needs about $130k of 30-day notional (~$4.2k/day), and Platinum needs ~$670k (~$22k/day). The rebate is therefore not a floor under the strategy at small size — it is a scale reward that most accounts never reach.

08

What this means for the bot

DSP-v2.5 trades clips far smaller than this wallet's current size, which puts it on the favourable part of the curve — if the curve generalises.

  • Benchmark against the small-size regime, not the current one.The wallet at $750/market earns roughly zero. The bot at $30–50/market is not competing with that number; it is competing with the wallet's July and August, when the same strategy earned 300–500 bps.
  • Validate at fixed size for at least 8 weeks.Because the edge is a function of size, any sample whose size changes measures an average size, not the bot. Eight weeks at a constant clip resolves a 30 bps question.
  • Model the rebate at your tier, not at the wallet's.The 123 bps figure was earned at Obsidian. A small account earns Bronze or Silver, where the same fee base returns 7–19 bps. Booking Obsidian rebates into a $1,000-bankroll plan overstates the strategy by an order of magnitude.
  • Watch the tier, not the strategy.Rebate yield moves with 30-day weighted volume, so a quiet month drops the rate even if nothing about the trading changed. That is a revenue cliff with no relationship to performance.
  • Expect the directional component to be variance, not income.It averages +$4.39/market across 30k markets but flips sign by month. Size and cap against it; do not forecast it.
09

Method and limits

The pull script caps a request at 7 days and 100 rows per page, so the history is 17 tiled weekly windows merged on full fill identity.

  • Integrity1,191,338 rows: 1,135,104 trades, 55,983 redemptions, 225 daily rebate rows. Zero duplicates across window boundaries.
  • Coverage31,506 markets over 114 days ≈ 276/day, about 96% of available 5-minute slots. 1,767 markets unresolved at export (5.6%).
  • EstimatorOLS through the origin of daily net P&L on daily notional. Volume weighting is automatic; a thin Sunday contributes little information.
  • Not identifiable hereCapacity versus competition: size and calendar time are collinear across all 17 weeks.
  • Not in the dataUnfilled quotes, queue position, intra-window mark-to-market, adverse selection beyond the next same-token fill.
  • The rebate ruleA tiered share of the prior day's taker fees set by 30-day weighted volume: 3 / 8 / 18 / 32 / 44 / 50%. Within a fixed tier the rate is exact to the cent; across the sample it moved four times as volume grew. Maker rebates are a separate pool of 15–25% of collected taker fees by category.
