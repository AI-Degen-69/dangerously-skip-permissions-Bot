## 1. Executive summary

- **What the strategy is.** A near-continuous, two-sided 5-minute BTC Up/Down scalper. It traded **1,912 of 2,016 available 5-minute slots (94.8%)** over seven days, bought **both sides in 1,839 of 1,912 markets (96.2%)**, entered at a median of **161 seconds before close**, clipped a modal **30 shares**, kept a median **$258** per market, and held every position to resolution — there are **zero SELL rows**.
- **Measured edge.** The first fill of the first-traded side beat its price by **+4.40pp ± 1.10pp** (t ≈ 4.0, n = 1,871). The first fill of the second-traded side was approximately fair: **−0.01pp ± 1.07pp** (n = 1,837; −0.31 to +0.07pp across tie-breaks). That first-touch edge is the only statistically robust anomaly in the file. The first fill alone realized **+$1,117.99 net (+5.33% on $20,979.90)**; the rest of the first-side position added **+$5,694.44**; the second side lost **−$7,039.27** (same-second ties broken by file order; §H gives the sensitivity range).
- **Hit rate.** Share-weighted hit rate **49.88%**; dollar-weighted **58.83%**; Down base rate **51.20%**; final market-lean hit rate **49.49%**. The final share imbalance is *anti*-predictive even though the first entry is predictive; the over-hedging/averaging-down pattern behind it is directionally present but weak (§B).
- **Capital efficiency.** Cash out **$724,797.13**, cash in **$723,553.64**, net trading **−$226.84 resolved (−0.03%)** / **−$1,243.49 all markets (−0.17%)**. Gross trading was **+$14,098.86 resolved (+1.95%)**, but inferred execution cost was **$14,347.02 (1.98% of cash out)**. Rebates of **$8,351.74** turn the week into **+$7,108.25 (+0.98% of cash out)**. The rebate is the profit; the trading book is roughly break-even.
- **Biggest risk.** The PnL is an **$8.35k rebate attached to a ~0% trading edge**. If the rebate rate falls by more than ~1.1% of volume, or the first-touch edge is in-sample noise, the week is flat-to-negative. Secondary risks: 41 unresolved markets ($1,016.65) are marked at zero; 81 exact-duplicate rows ($1,295.95) account for nearly the entire net loss; and the last two days show a sharp deleveraging (mean market cost $542 on Nov 4 → $141 on Nov 7), so the 7-day run-rate is not stable.

## 2. Data integrity & method

### 2.1 What was verified

The file contains exactly one wallet and four row types:

| Type | Rows | Meaning in this file |
|---|---:|---|
| `TRADE` | 81,138 | BUY fills only; no SELL rows |
| `REDEEM` | 3,708 | Settlement claims; one per token, winners pay ≈ shares, losers pay 0 |
| `MAKER_REBATE` | 7 | Daily maker incentive income, no market attached |
| `TAKER_REBATE` | 7 | Daily taker incentive income, no market attached |
| **Total** | **84,860** | |

- **One wallet:** `0x0ca4e9d2…` on every row. **1,912 markets, 3,751 tokens, 80,738 transaction hashes.**
- **Clock from the slug:** `close_ts = int(Market_Slug.rsplit("-", 1)[-1]) + 300`. Every trade lands in **`seconds_before_close` ∈ [11, 297]`**; **0 rows outside [0, 300]**. The bot's active window is therefore `open + 3s` to `close − 11s`.
- **The CSV is newest-first, not chronological.** The first data row is `2026-11-08T23:38:45Z`. Every `first()`/`last()` aggregation must sort by `Timestamp_UTC` first; otherwise "first fill" is actually the last fill. This report uses sorted aggregations throughout. The one place where the original script did not sort was section H; that bug was found and fixed, and all first/second-side numbers below are post-fix. Second-resolution ties remain genuinely ambiguous in 79 markets; §H quantifies the range.
- **Resolution is recoverable and clean:** 1,871 winning `REDEEM` rows, 1,837 losing rows, **zero markets with two winners**, winning-token payout ratio **exactly 1.0** on every row (min = median = max = 1.0), losing rows **exactly 0**. The 34 markets with only one `REDEEM` row are all winners; no settled market has zero winners.
- **Redeem lag is short and uniform:** median **91s**, mean **90.4s**, p99 **97s**, max **210s**; **no redeem lag above 1 hour**. The 41 unresolved markets are therefore not "pending redemption" — the file simply has no resolution rows for them.
- **Prices are valid probabilities:** min **0.05**, max **0.96**, zero rows at or below 0. Median 0.47, p1 0.08, p99 0.91.
- **Duplicates:** 81 exact duplicate **extra** rows on `(Transaction_Hash, Token_ID, Price, Shares)` — 81 groups of exactly two rows, 162 rows involved, **$1,295.95** of extra cash out and **2,405** extra shares. A wider key `(tx, token, price)` flags 237 rows, but the extra 156 are same-price/different-share fills that may be legitimate partial fills. Headline PnL keeps all 81 duplicates; the deduplicated alternative is quantified below.
- **Effective price vs quoted price:** `USD_Amount / Shares` differs from `Price` by more than 1% on **50.17%** of fills. This is the file's only execution-cost signal; see §2.2.

### 2.2 Truth gaps and assumptions

The prompt's non-negotiable rules require labeling what the file cannot show. The file has **no fee column, no order-book snapshots, no cancel data, no mark-to-market, no BTC spot series, and no SELL rows**. Every PnL below is therefore a **hold-to-resolution, cash-in/cash-out** PnL, not a mark-to-market PnL.

**Inferred execution cost.** I define `exec_cost = USD_Amount − Shares × Price`, and call it *inferred* because the CSV never labels it. It is **$14,347.02**, or **1.98% of cash out**:

| Statistic | Value |
|---|---:|
| Fills with positive markup | 42,323 / 81,138 (**52.16%**) |
| Fills with zero markup | 38,714 (**47.72%**) |
| Fills with negative markup | 101, all rounding-level (sum ≈ $0.00) |
| Total inferred cost | **$14,347.02** |
| Cost as % of cash out | **1.98%** |
| Markup as % of USD, median | **1.04%** |
| Markup as % of USD, p95 / max | **5.30% / 6.24%** |

Of the 42,323 positive-markup fills, **42,248 are in resolved markets and 75 are in unresolved markets**; the taker-like row in section I is resolved-only.

The markup is **not a flat fee**. Among positive-markup fills it is about **6.0%** of USD at prices below 0.10, **3.5%** at 0.45-0.50, and **0.5%** at prices above 0.90; in cents per share it peaks near mid prices (~1.7¢ at 0.50) and falls at both tails. That shape is consistent with **crossing a bid-ask spread**, not a fixed commission. Without an order book or a fee column, the split between fee and spread is **NOT DETERMINABLE FROM THIS FILE**. I use "maker-like" for zero-markup fills and "taker-like" for positive-markup fills, and I do **not** call the markup a fee.

**Unresolved markets.** 41 markets have no `REDEEM` row: **$1,016.65 cash out, 287 fills, 3,898.1 shares**. They span the whole week (first close 2026-11-02 08:00 UTC, last close 2026-11-08 23:40 UTC), so they are not just an end-of-file artifact. Headline accounting marks them at **zero payout**, the conservative choice. Sensitivity: if they were voided at $0.50/share, they would return **$1,949.05**, turning all-market net trading from **−$1,243.49** into **+$705.56**. There is no evidence for a $0.50 payout in the file; the zero assumption is the only defensible one, and the $0.50 case is a labeled hypothetical.

**Duplicates.** The 81 duplicate rows are exact matches on transaction hash, token, price, and shares. They may be export artifacts or genuine separate fills. Keeping them, resolved net trading is **−$226.84**; deduplicating them gives **−$5.69**. The duplicates are therefore **$221.15 of the $226.84 net loss**. Both numbers are reported; the headline keeps them, because dropping rows is a stronger assumption than keeping them.

**Rebates.** The 14 rebate rows are daily payments at ~00:10 (`TAKER_REBATE`) and ~00:45 (`MAKER_REBATE`) from Nov 2 through Nov 8. They carry no `Condition_ID`, `Outcome`, `Price`, or market slug; `Shares` equals the rebate amount and `Price` is 0. They are **income, not trading edge**, and are reported separately everywhere. Total **$8,351.74** = **$7,124.11 taker (85.3%)** + **$1,227.62 maker (14.7%)**. The last trading day's rebate (for Nov 8) would be paid on Nov 9, outside the file, so the 7-day total is a slight **understatement** of the rebate stream.

### 2.3 Reconciliation

> **Cash reconciliation.** TRADE cash out **$724,797.13** − REDEEM cash in **$723,553.64** = **−$1,243.49**. The synthetic per-market hold-to-resolution PnL sums to the same **−$1,243.49** (difference **$0.00**). Gross PnL **+$13,103.54** − inferred execution cost **$14,347.02** = **−$1,243.48** (rounding $0.01). Resolved-only: cash out **$723,780.48**, cash in **$723,553.64**, net **−$226.84**; gross **+$14,098.86** − resolved execution cost **$14,325.69** = **−$226.83** (rounding $0.01). The reconciliation is exact to rounding; there is no unexplained cash leak.

The two PnL conventions used below:

- **All markets:** every TRADE is included; unresolved markets pay 0. This is the conservative, cash-complete view.
- **Resolved only:** only the 1,871 markets with a `REDEEM` row. This is the clean strategy view, but it silently drops $1,016.65 of unresolved cost if read alone.

## 4. The calibration table (highlighted)

This is the highest-value section. The prompt asks: **conditional on buying at price p, how often did that token actually win?** The answer depends on *which* price you use. There are three candidates, and only one is a clean entry-price test:

1. **First-fill price** — the price of the first fill on each token. This is the closest thing to an exogenous entry price; the bot had not yet influenced the token's path. **This is the clean test.**
2. **Average price** — total cost / total shares. This is **path-dependent**: a token that wins tends to have a high average price because the bot bought early and the price rose; a token that loses tends to have a low average price because the bot bought early and the price fell. It is an *outcome* of the path, not an input. **Do not use it as evidence of edge.**
3. **Marginal fill price** — each individual fill's price vs the token's eventual outcome. This tests whether the market is efficient fill-by-fill. It is a useful cross-check, but it is not an entry-timing test.

### 4.5 Calibration verdict

- **The only robust anomaly is the first fill of the first-traded side:** +4.40pp ± 1.10pp, t ≈ 4.0, n = 1,871. It is positive in every 0.10-wide bucket from 0.1 to 0.8 except the noisy 0.8–0.9 tail.
- **The second side is the control:** −0.01pp ± 1.07pp. The edge is not a price-level effect.
- **The average-price table is a path-dependence trap:** it shows extreme anti-calibration because the average price is determined by the outcome path. It must not be used as evidence of edge.
- **The fill-level table is roughly diagonal:** +0.97pp overall, with small positive/negative deviations. The market is not systematically mispriced fill-by-fill.
- **The first-touch edge decays:** the first fill alone has a +5.33% net ROI; the rest of the first-side position has +1.59%; the second side has −2.04%. This is consistent with a **transient first-touch/latency premium** or a **short-lived predictive signal**, not with a persistent directional forecast. The file cannot distinguish the two mechanisms.

## 5. Strategy specification

### 5.1 Prose

The bot is a **two-sided 5-minute BTC Up/Down scalper** that mixes taker-like and maker-like fills (52.2% / 47.7% of fills; 69.1% / 30.9% of cash out). It trades almost every slot (94.8%), buys both sides in almost every market (96.2%), and never sells. Its first fill is its best fill: the first-traded side wins 54.5% at a 50.1% average price. It then adds to that side on adverse moves and buys the other side as a hedge, usually 15 seconds later. The hedge is fair at first touch but becomes a drag as the bot pays up; the realized matched pair costs 1.004, not the 0.961 seen at first touch. The gross book makes +$14.1k, the inferred execution cost is $14.3k, and the rebate is $8.35k. The strategy is a **rebate farm with a small, real first-touch edge**, not a pure directional predictor and not a pure arbitrage.

The bot also has two identifiable risk behaviors:
1. **Multi-hour pauses after negative 2h PnL** — all five long outages followed a negative two-hour window (p ≈ 1.6% under a 44% base rate).
2. **Multi-day deleveraging after the Nov 4 drawdown** — mean market cost fell from $542 (Nov 4) to $141 (Nov 7), and median clip size fell from $6.30 to $3.93.

Both are consistent with a risk manager, but the exact triggers are not in the file.

## 7. Falsifiable predictions

These are concrete numbers the same strategy should produce on a fresh 24-hour window (≈250–290 markets). They are stated so a reader can falsify the reconstruction.

1. **First-side first-fill edge:** on a fresh window, the first-traded side should beat its first-fill price by **+2pp to +7pp** (point estimate +4.4pp; 95% CI at 250 markets is wide, roughly ±6pp). If the measured edge is ≤0 or ≥+10pp, the first-touch story is wrong.
2. **Second-side first-fill edge:** should be **within ±2pp of zero**. If the second side shows a >2pp edge, the "only the first touch has alpha" conclusion is wrong.
3. **Realized pair cost:** the average realized pair cost should be **1.00 ± 0.01**, even though the first-fill pair cost is ~0.96. If the realized pair cost is <0.99, the hedge is cheaper than this week and the strategy is more profitable than measured.
4. **Rebate share:** rebates should be **0.8%–1.2% of cash out** (this week 1.15%). If rebates fall below 0.5%, the strategy is likely negative; if they exceed 1.5%, the rebate regime has changed.
5. **Timing and sizing fingerprint:** median entry **161s ± 20s** before close; modal clip **30 shares**; median market cost **$258 ± 30%**; both sides in **94%–98%** of markets. A large shift in any of these means the regime or the operator has changed.

**What would make the reconstruction underdetermined:** if a fresh window shows the first-side edge but *not* the second-side flatness, or if the rebate share and the first-side edge move in opposite directions, the simple "first-touch alpha + rebate farm" model is insufficient. The file cannot distinguish a predictive signal from a transient liquidity/latency premium, so a fresh window with BTC spot data is the right next test.

## 9. Reproduction

**Run:**

```bash
python docs/leaderboard-traders/scripts/analyze_goodfallen.py
```

The script reads `goodfallen_2026-11-01_to_2026-11-08.csv` from the same folder by default and prints every table in this report (sections A–J). It requires `pandas` and `numpy` only. The snippet below is a simplified derivation; the script is authoritative for one-sided markets, unresolved markets, duplicate rows, and the exposure/float calculations.

**Core derivation (the parts a reader needs to re-derive the numbers):**

```python
import pandas as pd, numpy as np

raw = pd.read_csv("goodfallen_2026-11-01_to_2026-11-08.csv", low_memory=False)
raw["ts"] = pd.to_datetime(raw.Timestamp_UTC, utc=True)

tr  = raw[raw.Type == "TRADE"].copy()
rd  = raw[raw.Type == "REDEEM"].copy()
reb = raw[raw.Type.str.endswith("REBATE")].copy()

# Clock from the slug; the CSV is newest-first, so always sort before first()/last().
tr["slug_ts"] = tr.Market_Slug.str.rsplit("-", n=1).str[-1].astype(int)
tr["close_ts"] = pd.to_datetime(tr.slug_ts + 300, unit="s", utc=True)
tr["sbc"] = (tr.close_ts - tr.ts).dt.total_seconds()
tr["eff"] = tr.USD_Amount / tr.Shares
tr["exec_cost"] = tr.USD_Amount - tr.Shares * tr.Price

# Resolution: the REDEEM row with USD_Amount > 0 is the winning token.
win = rd[rd.USD_Amount > 0].groupby("Condition_ID").Outcome.first()

# Per-token aggregation (sort first: the raw file is newest-first).
tr = tr.sort_values(["Condition_ID", "ts"])
tok = tr.groupby(["Condition_ID", "Outcome"]).agg(
    shares=("Shares", "sum"), cost=("USD_Amount", "sum"),
    first_p=("Price", "first"), last_p=("Price", "last"),
    n=("Price", "size"), first_ts=("ts", "first"), last_ts=("ts", "last"),
).reset_index()
tok["winner"] = tok.Condition_ID.map(win)
tok["hit"] = (tok.Outcome == tok.winner).astype(float)
tok["pnl_net"] = tok.shares * tok.hit - tok.cost

# Per-market PnL (unresolved markets pay 0).
m = tr.groupby("Condition_ID").agg(
    cost=("USD_Amount", "sum"), shares=("Shares", "sum"),
    slug_ts=("slug_ts", "first"), first_ts=("ts", "min"), last_ts=("ts", "max"),
).reset_index()
m["winner"] = m.Condition_ID.map(win)
m["payout"] = m.Condition_ID.map(rd.groupby("Condition_ID").USD_Amount.sum())
m["pnl_net"] = m.payout.fillna(0) - m.cost

# First side = side of the earliest fill in the market.
ff = tr.groupby("Condition_ID").first().reset_index()
first_side = ff.set_index("Condition_ID").Outcome

# First-fill calibration: bucket by first_p, compare hit to first_p.
tok["side"] = np.where(tok.Outcome == tok.Condition_ID.map(first_side), "first", "second")
resolved = tok[tok.winner.notna()].copy()
resolved["b"] = pd.cut(resolved.first_p, np.arange(0, 1.01, 0.05))
cal = resolved[resolved.side == "first"].groupby("b", observed=True).apply(
    lambda x: pd.Series({
        "n": len(x), "avg_p": x.first_p.mean(), "hit": x.hit.mean(),
        "edge": x.hit.mean() - x.first_p.mean(),
        "cost": x.cost.sum(), "pnl_net": x.pnl_net.sum(),
    }), include_groups=False)

# Pair / direction decomposition.
piv = tok.sort_values(["Condition_ID", "Outcome", "first_ts"]).pivot(
    index="Condition_ID", columns="Outcome", values=["shares", "cost"])
piv.columns = ["_".join(c) for c in piv.columns]
piv["M"] = piv[["shares_Up", "shares_Down"]].min(axis=1)
piv["pair_net"] = piv.M * (1 - piv.cost_Up / piv.shares_Up - piv.cost_Down / piv.shares_Down)
```

The script's section H was fixed to sort by `Timestamp_UTC` before `groupby("Condition_ID").first()`. Without that sort, the "first side" is actually the last fill, and the first/second decomposition is wrong (it produced +$39.5k / −$39.7k instead of +$6.8k / −$7.0k under the file-order tie-break). If you re-derive from the raw CSV, **sort first**.
