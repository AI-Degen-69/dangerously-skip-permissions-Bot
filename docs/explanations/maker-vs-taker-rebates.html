Maker Rebates vs. Taker Rebates

A visual breakdown of how liquidity rewards, order routing incentives, and fee structures work on Polymarket's Central Limit Order Book (CLOB).

Liquidity Provider (Passive)

Maker Rebate

A maker order adds liquidity to the orderbook. It sits as a resting limit order (bid below ask, or ask above bid) waiting for someone else to fill it.

Why exchanges pay Maker Rebates:

  • Deep Orderbooks: Polymarket needs tight spreads so retail users don't suffer huge slippage.
  • Adverse Selection Compensation: Makers carry inventory risk and risk getting filled right before price collapses.
  • Polymarket Liquidity Rewards: Distributed daily based on order proximity to midpoint and size.
Typical Formula & Flow:
Rebate = Size × Spread Proximity Multiplier
Recorded on-chain as MAKER_REBATE.
Liquidity Consumer (Aggressor)

Taker Rebate

A taker order removes liquidity from the orderbook. It matches immediately with existing resting orders (using Market, FAK, or IOC orders).

Why exchanges pay Taker Rebates:

  • Volume Tiers / VIP Incentives: High-frequency funds and top-tier bots (e.g. Obsidian Tier) generate massive fee volume for the protocol.
  • Order Flow Kickbacks: Exchanges kick back a portion of fees or sponsor fee-free tiers with negative net fees to attract top active traders.
  • Fee Subsidies: Certain fast binary markets (like 5m BTC) offer negative taker fee incentives to stimulate active turnover.
